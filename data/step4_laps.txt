Step 4 - Lap Count

1. Lap definition: A lap starts when the car pulls away from rest at the
   start area and ends when it crosses back over the start-line longitude.
   One lap = launching from the start area, driving to the far end of the
   straight, turning around, and coming back over the start line.

   Why "pulls away from rest" instead of "crosses the start-line longitude":
   before Lap 2 the car was parked about 14 m behind the start line
   (longitude -121.733353 vs. the start line at about -121.733200). It did
   its whole launch (0 -> ~11.7 m/s) before reaching the line, and only
   crossed it at ~99.95 s. Using the longitude crossing would cut the launch
   and the top speed out of Lap 2. Starting each lap when the car leaves
   rest captures the full launch for both laps.

   How the start times were found: the last time speed was ~0 (<= 0.1 m/s)
   before each launch, checked against the pedal (throttle goes above 0
   right before each launch).
     - Lap 1: car last at rest at ~20.1 s (pedal starts rising ~19-20 s)
     - Lap 2: car last at rest at ~94.8 s (pedal starts rising ~94.4 s)

2. Lap count: 1.5 laps

3. Lap times:
   Lap 1: 20 - 68 s
   Lap 2: 95 - 133 s
   Note: Lap 2 never crosses back over the start line, so it ends when
   the car comes to a stop.

4. Side note: the GPS longitude barely changes during the first few
   seconds of each launch even though the car is moving (e.g. it stays at
   -121.733353 from 85 s to ~96 s). The GPS updates slowly / has limited
   resolution, so speed (from motor RPM) is more reliable than GPS for
   finding exactly when the car starts moving.
