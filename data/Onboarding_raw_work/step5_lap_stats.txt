Step 5 - Lap Stats (max speed, max acceleration, time accelerating, time coasting)

Lap windows (from Step 4):
   Lap 1: 20 - 68 s
   Lap 2: 95 - 133 s

Method for selecting each lap:
   Built a True/False mask for each lap, (time >= start) & (time <= end),
   and used it to filter both the time and speed columns. Because both
   columns are filtered with the same mask, they still line up row for row.
   Checked: the first/last filtered times match the window, and the time
   and speed columns have the same length.

   Speed is in m/s, converted from SME_TRQSPD_Speed (motor RPM):
   speed = RPM * gear ratio (12/41) * 2 * pi * wheel radius (0.2 m) / 60

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Max speed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Lap 1: 10.54 m/s  (~37.9 km/h), at ~25 s
   Lap 2: 11.68 m/s  (~42.1 km/h), at ~99.0 s

   Method: .max() on each lap's filtered speed column. Checked against the
   speed vs. time graph (peaks near 25 s and just before 100 s).

   Note: with the old Lap 2 start (100 s), the max was only 10.43 m/s,
   because the real peak at ~99 s was outside the window. Moving the start
   to 95 s (see Step 4) fixed this.

   Note: this is WHEEL speed (from motor RPM), which equals car speed only
   if the tires grip. Lap 2's peak lands on a wiggle in the speed trace
   right after a hard launch (63% pedal), likely wheelspin and the
   drivetrain winding up and springing back, so .max() picks the top of a
   wobble. A 0.5 s average gives 10.83 m/s and lag-corrected GPS gives
   ~11.0 m/s, so the car's true top speed was probably closer to 11 m/s
   (~6-7% lower). Lap 1 is barely affected (0.5 s average: 10.30 m/s).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Max acceleration
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
   FINAL: speed-based values (0.5 s window), checked against the IMU -
   see "IMU check" at the end of this section.

   Lap 1: 6.66 m/s^2 (~0.68 g), at ~51.6 s (row 2657)   [n = 42, 0.5 s]
   Lap 2: 7.13 m/s^2 (~0.73 g), at ~98.3 s (row 281)    [n = 42, 0.5 s]
          (n = 21 gave 8.98 m/s^2 (~0.92 g), at ~98.0 s (row 250))

   Method: a = dv/dt, using .diff(n) on both the lap's speed and time
   columns, then .max() and .arg_max() to find the peak and when it happened.

   First attempt (wrong): .diff() with n = 1 (row to row, 0.0119 s apart)
   gave 92.4 m/s^2 (~9.4 g) for Lap 1, which is not physically possible.
   Printing the rows around the spike (~51.38 s) showed why:
     - The speed sensor only updates every 2nd row. Forward-fill copies the
       old value in between, so every other row has dv = 0 and the whole
       change lands in a single 0.0119 s step.
     - One outlier reading (5.805 -> 6.902 m/s in one step, ~6x larger than
       the jumps around it, with speed dropping slightly right before and
       after) became the max.
     - Dividing by such a small dt multiplies any noise by ~84.
   Over the 0.24 s around the spike, speed went 5.456 -> 7.423 m/s, about
   8.3 m/s^2, so the car really was accelerating hard there, just not 92.

   Fix: take the difference over a longer window, .diff(n) with n rows
   (~84 rows per second). Lap 1 results:
     n = 21 (~0.25 s): 9.19 m/s^2 (~0.94 g), row 2658
     n = 42 (~0.5 s):  6.66 m/s^2 (~0.68 g), row 2657
     n = 84 (~1 s):    4.38 m/s^2 (~0.45 g), row 2657
   All three land at the same spot (~51.6 s, the steep rise on the speed
   graph), so it's a real event, not noise. The value drops as the window
   grows because the hard push is short, and longer windows average in
   slower parts.

   Decision: use 0.5 s (n = 42) for both laps. It is long
   enough to average out the forward-filled samples and the outlier, but
   short enough not to flatten the real push. The true peak is roughly in
   the 4.4 - 9.2 m/s^2 range depending on the window.

   IMU check:
   Setup:
     - VDM_X_AXIS_ACCELERATION points BACKWARDS (positive = slowing), so
       forward acceleration = -X * 9.81 m/s^2.
     - Offset: during the clean coast at 26-40 s, the IMU read 0.317 m/s^2
       (0.032 g) more negative than the slope of the speed graph. Likely
       the sensor is mounted with a slight tilt. Added +0.317 to correct it.
       The offset isn't perfectly constant (other coasts are off by 0.05 -
       0.14 m/s^2), so the IMU is good to about +/- 0.1 m/s^2.
     - Averaged the IMU over the same windows as the speed method
       (rolling_mean(n) vs .diff(n)) so they're compared fairly.

   Results (m/s^2):
                    speed-based (motor RPM)    IMU (corrected)
     Lap 1, 0.25 s:  9.19 at 51.61 s          5.48 at 35.48 s (*)
     Lap 1, 0.5 s:   6.66 at 51.59 s          4.52 at 51.83 s
     Lap 1, 1 s:     4.38 at 51.59 s          3.33 at 52.05 s
     Lap 2, 0.25 s:  8.98 at 97.98 s          4.83 at 98.91 s
     Lap 2, 0.5 s:   7.13 at 98.35 s          4.38 at 98.91 s
     Lap 2, 1 s:     5.53 at 98.69 s          3.77 at 99.00 s
     (*) a bump/vibration spike with no pedal and no speed change, not a
         real push. Short IMU windows pick up bumps.

   What it shows:
     - Timing agrees: both methods put the peak in the same push (~51.6 -
       51.8 s in Lap 1, ~98 - 99 s in Lap 2).
     - Size doesn't: at 0.5 s the IMU peak is ~2.1 m/s^2 (Lap 1) and
       ~2.8 m/s^2 (Lap 2) lower.
     - Over whole pushes the two agree better: Lap 1 launch (20.1-24.6 s)
       averages 2.22 from speed vs 2.37 from IMU. The gap is mainly at the
       short, hard peaks.

   Likely reason: motor RPM measures how fast the WHEELS spin, the IMU
   measures how fast the CAR speeds up. Under hard throttle (57-63% pedal
   at both peaks) the rear wheels can spin a little faster than the car is
   moving (wheelspin), and the speed trace has visible wiggles right at
   both peaks (driveline oscillation). Both make the wheels look like they
   accelerate harder than the car does. Not proven: GPS speed updates only
   every ~0.24 s and lags ~0.9 s, so it's too coarse to confirm.

   Decision: report the speed-based values (6.66 / 7.13 m/s^2). This
   uses SME_TRQSPD_Speed like the rest of the project, so it matches max
   speed and the accelerating/coasting definitions. Keep in mind it's
   wheel acceleration; the IMU says the car body itself peaked lower
   (4.52 / 4.38 m/s^2).

3. Time spent accelerating
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Lap 1: 9.90 s
            20.00 - 24.82 s  (4.82 s)   launch (period started at 19.81, clipped)
            41.41 - 42.96 s  (1.56 s)   light pedal (~7%), 4.3 -> 4.5 m/s
            47.11 - 48.57 s  (1.46 s)   light pedal (~8%), 3.8 -> 4.3 m/s
            50.22 - 52.28 s  (2.07 s)   hard push (up to 59%), 3.4 -> 7.7 m/s
   Lap 2: 4.67 s
            95.00 - 99.67 s  (4.67 s)   launch (period started at 94.33, clipped)

   Definition: accelerating = the car is GAINING SPEED (dv/dt > 0), not
   "pedal > 0". Light pedal can just hold speed or still slow down, so
   pedal shows what the driver asks for, not what the car does.

   Method:
     1. dv/dt over a 0.5 s window (.diff(42), same as max acceleration),
        computed on the whole run so each lap's first 0.5 s isn't lost.
        Row-to-row (.diff(1)) was too noisy: it marked 17% of a pure
        coast (26-40 s) as speeding up.
     2. find_periods on (dv/dt > 0): gaps under 0.1 s merged, periods
        under 0.7 s dropped. The junk periods were all under 0.2 s and the
        shortest real push is ~1 s, so the result doesn't depend on the
        exact cutoff.
     3. Clipped each period to the lap window, skipped any fully outside
        the lap, and added up (end - start).

   Caveat: merging gaps pulls 41.41 - 41.94 s into the ~42 s push even
   though speed dropped slightly there, and stretches the 50.22 / 52.28 s
   edges, so Lap 1 is roughly 0.5 - 1 s high.

4. Time spent coasting
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Lap 1: 37.18 s
            24.63 - 41.18 s  (16.55 s)
            42.88 - 46.41 s  ( 3.53 s)
            48.63 - 49.53 s  ( 0.90 s)
            51.80 - 68.00 s  (16.20 s)   (clipped at lap end)
   Lap 2: 32.83 s
            99.33 - 103.03 s  ( 3.70 s)
           103.83 - 132.96 s  (29.13 s)

   Definition: coasting = no throttle (pedal == 0) AND no brake (brake
   voltage <= 375) AND moving (speed > 0.1 m/s). Coasting is about what
   the driver is doing, not what the speed is doing: a car rolling
   downhill with nothing pressed is still coasting even if it speeds up,
   so "not gaining speed" is NOT part of the definition. (Checked: with
   pedal 0, speed never rose for more than 0.28 s, so no hill here was
   steep enough to matter.)

   Method: same as time accelerating. find_periods on the coasting mask
   (gaps under 0.1 s merged, periods under 0.7 s dropped), clipped each
   period to the lap window, added up (end - start).

   Notes:
     - The brake is never pressed during either lap (brake voltage max
       372, under the 375 threshold), so "no brake" removes nothing here,
       but it stays in the definition. ETC_STATUS_BRAKELIGHT was not used:
       it reads 1 for 95% of the run, so it isn't reliable.
     - The Lap 2 gap at 103.03 - 103.83 s is the driver tapping the pedal
       (up to ~42%) a few times. Speed kept dropping, so it isn't
       accelerating either; it's correctly left out of coasting.
     - Accelerating and coasting overlap slightly: Lap 1 by ~0.75 s
       (24.63-24.82, 42.88-42.96, 51.80-52.28) and Lap 2 by ~0.34 s
       (99.33-99.67). dv/dt uses the PREVIOUS 0.5 s, so an accelerating
       period keeps going a little after the pedal is released. A centered
       window would shrink this.
     - Accelerating + coasting don't add up to the whole lap: rows with
       the pedal pressed but the car not gaining speed (holding speed)
       belong to neither.
