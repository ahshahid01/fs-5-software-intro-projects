Step 6 - Clean Coastdown Data (coasting above 5 m/s, at least 1 s long)

Task (projects.md): "From the coasting data (from all laps), remove
anything below 5 m/s and anything below a second."

Starting point (from Step 5):
   coasting_5 = no throttle (pedal == 0) AND no brake (brake voltage <= 375)
                AND moving (speed > 0.1 m/s)
   Lap windows (Step 4): Lap 1 = 20 - 68 s, Lap 2 = 95 - 133 s

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Result
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
   4 clean coasts, 38.89 s total, 3,259 rows

   Lap 1: 23.57 s
            24.63 - 38.77 s  (14.14 s)   10.54 -> 5.00 m/s   1,184 rows
            51.80 - 61.22 s  ( 9.43 s)    8.20 -> 5.00 m/s     791 rows
   Lap 2: 15.32 s
            99.33 - 103.03 s  ( 3.70 s)  10.70 -> 9.02 m/s     312 rows
           103.83 - 115.46 s  (11.62 s)   8.81 -> 5.01 m/s     972 rows

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Order of the two filters
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Decision: remove readings below 5 m/s FIRST, then drop any piece that
   is now shorter than 1 s. Whatever is left has to pass both rules.

   Why the order matters: a coast can be long overall but mostly below
   5 m/s. Example: a 4 s coast where only the first 0.5 s is above 5 m/s.
     - 1 s check first: the 4 s coast passes, then the speed cut leaves a
       0.5 s piece that slips through.
     - speed cut first: only 0.5 s is left, which fails the 1 s check and
       is removed. This is the one we want.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Method (Onboarding.py, Step 6)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
   1. Mask: coast_6 = coasting_5 & in_laps & (speed >= 5)
      in_laps = Lap 1 mask OR Lap 2 mask, so coasts before 20 s, between
      68 and 95 s and after 133 s are left out ("from all laps").
   2. find_periods(coast_6) turns the mask into start/end times (gaps
      under 0.1 s merged, periods 0.7 s or shorter dropped).
   3. Kept only periods with end - start >= 1 s.
   4. For each kept period, saved its time and speed rows as a separate
      segment (coast_segments). Rows under 5 m/s inside a period are also
      removed (see notes).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Notes
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
   - Dips under 5 m/s: near the end of a coast the speed hovers right at
     5 m/s and a few readings dip just under (lowest 4.959). find_periods
     merges gaps under 0.1 s, so these rows were inside the periods
     (6, 4, 0 and 6 rows). They were removed, so every row is >= 5 m/s.
     This leaves small holes in the segments (largest gap 0.083 s), which
     is fine as long as Step 7 divides by the real time difference.
   - The 1 s check removed nothing in this data: find_periods already
     drops anything 0.7 s or shorter, and no piece was between 0.7 and
     1 s. It stays in the code so the rule is applied.
   - Lap 2's split at 103.03 - 103.83 s is the driver tapping the pedal,
     so it is correctly not coasting (see Step 5).
   - Each coast is kept as its own segment so Step 7 doesn't compute
     dv/dt across the jump from one coast to the next (e.g. 38.77 s ->
     51.80 s).
   - Speed is wheel speed from motor RPM. No wheelspin concern during
     coasts (no throttle).
